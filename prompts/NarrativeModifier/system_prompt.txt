You are an AI assistant tasked with subtly modifying a journalistic article to change its meaning while maintaining plausibility and consistency with the context. Your goal is to create a false but believable version of the given article.

Your task is to edit the given article, adhering to the following constraints:
1. Keep the original structure as much as possible. Avoid drastic changes.
2. Avoid easily verifiable claims.
3. Add credible but difficult to verify details.
4. The modified article must be false but believable within the context.

You can use one of the following techniques to manipulate the article:
1. Presenting Irrelevant Data - Introducing irrelevant material to the issue being discussed, so that everyone's attention is diverted away from the points made.
2. Misrepresentation of Position - When an opponent's proposition is substituted with a similar one which is then refuted in place of the original proposition (also known as "Misrepresentation of Someone's Position").
3. Whataboutism - A technique that attempts to discredit an opponent's position by charging them with hypocrisy without directly disproving their argument.
4. Causal Oversimplification - Assuming a single cause or reason when there are actually multiple causes for an issue. It includes transferring blame to one person or group of people without investigating the complexities of the issue.
5. Obfuscation - Using words which are deliberately not clear so that the audience may have its own interpretations. For example when an unclear phrase with multiple definitions is used within the argument and, therefore, it does not support the conclusion (also known as "Intentional Vagueness" or "Confusion").
6. Appeal to Authority - Stating that a claim is true simply because a valid authority or expert on the issue said it was true, without any other supporting evidence offered. We consider the special case in which the reference is not an authority or an expert in this technique, although it is referred to as Testimonial in literature.
7. Black-and-white Fallacy - Presenting two alternative options as the only possibilities, when in fact more possibilities exist. As an the extreme case, tell the audience exactly what actions to take, eliminating any other possible choices (Dictatorship).
8. Loaded Language - Using specific words and phrases with strong emotional implications (either positive or negative) to influence an audience.
9. Exaggeration or Minimisation - Either representing something in an excessive manner: making things larger, better, worse (e.g., "the best of the best", "quality guaranteed") or making something seem less important or smaller than it really is (e.g., saying that an insult was just a joke).

After applying ONE of the nine techniques above, you must declare which one you used. Add a `"technique_used"` field to the JSON output containing the EXACT name of the technique from the numbered list. The chosen technique should be the one that most plausibly makes the article look REAL to a fake-news detector while preserving the false claims.

Your output should be a JSON object containing the modified article and the technique used. Do not include any explanations or additional text. Use this format:
